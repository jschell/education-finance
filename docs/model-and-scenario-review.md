## Judgment

**The monthly cash-flow engine is the right foundation. The current scenario family is appropriate as a controlled price-model laboratory, but too narrow to support a broad claim about a family's education funding resilience.** We have spent substantial effort testing distributions conditional on a fixed educational path and largely reliable support. Some omitted plan changes can alter the answer materially even with zero investment uncertainty.

The next priority should be to specify the actual funding decision and broaden the scenario/benchmark design, in parallel with a bounded price-source trace. Do not make every useful planning improvement wait for another residual correction or a perfectly calibrated 18-year probability. Equally, do not promote the present probability as a general household outcome by merely adding more scenario counts.

Established work is available. Education-specific lifecycle investing, multigoal asset–liability planning, structural time-series models and robust decision analysis all address parts of this problem. Reuse their components and problem formulations; none of the reviewed sources transfers a complete calibrated joint school/aid/income/portfolio model into our app. [Sources and limits](model-research-sources.md).

## 1. What is worth retaining

- **A dated monthly ledger and pure engine.** `engine.js` models deposits, gifts, grants, income, tuition timing and living costs, then records a first unpaid covered bill. An eventual recovery does not erase that missed payment. This is substantially more useful than testing only an account balance on enrollment day.
- **Student ownership and paired household worlds.** `domain.js`, `household.js` and `calibrationFactory` prevent money duplication, share economic draws and require every selected goal to be paid. Preserve earmarking; a general pooled-wealth model cannot replace it without explicit constraints.
- **Reproducible inputs and financial contracts.** Source/protocol/report hashes, failed-candidate retention, independent solver verification, immutable prospective records and source-quality audits are useful infrastructure.
- **Corrections already made.** The infeasible old gate, conditional equity mean mismatch and source-year/CPI timing were addressed in the overhaul. Reopening those exact defects as if model12 still shipped them would be inaccurate.
- **Honest non-promotion.** The coherent hierarchy lost; the residual candidate has only a limited research qualification. Keeping the foundation active was preferable to activating a better-looking but unsupported result.

These strengths support retaining the static, buildless app, one Calculate action, lean offline research and no live school scraping. This review does not propose a wholesale rewrite.

## 2. The question needs a stricter contract

The current result is approximately:

> Given the entered education path, dated funding schedule, covered-expense goal, asset policy and the selected economic model, what fraction of simulated paths pays every covered bill?

That is a useful **conditional funding calculation**. It is not automatically:

- the chance this student completes college;
- the chance the family can afford college while meeting all other obligations;
- a probability over future school choice, aid renewals, household income or attendance duration;
- proof that a stated target deposit is economically calibrated;
- an optimization over all feasible household deposit policies.

The first product-level choice should be explicit: is the goal to pre-fund all college costs, cover tuition only, provide a specified parent contribution, or compare an actual school offer? The engine supports several coverage contracts, but model evaluation predominantly uses a full tuition-plus-housing obligation. A precise probability for the wrong goal is not a useful improvement.

There are also four different things currently called a scenario: an education choice, an assumption case, a stochastic path and a stress. A fifth is a policy—the actions taken as information arrives. They need separate identities and meanings. Changing school is a choice, a recession is a possible world, and pausing deposits is an action or a household constraint; these are not interchangeable draws from one distribution.

| Eight-year-wait households | No observed complete outcomes | No supported whole-household reliability claim for that horizon |

These are raw support counts, not independent observations or the equal-origin performance estimates. A forecast of 1 from 400 paths is not certainty.

The common `schoolPlan`/`planFor` setup uses four attendance years for the sector comparisons, including public two-year sectors. That is a valid standardized four-year price experiment; it is not validation of a two-year credential or a two-plus-two transfer path. It also sets books, transport and other costs to zero, stops the base savings schedule before entry, uses fixed grant/income support and schedules a gift. Those deliberate controls should remain documented as controls, not presented as the full variety of real plans.

### Controlled examples show why the omitted dimensions matter

All examples below use synthetic inputs, zero investment returns and zero price growth; they demonstrate accounting sensitivity, not event probabilities.

1. **Attendance duration:** $100,000 funds four years at $25,000/year. Five years requires $125,000 and leaves a $25,000 unpaid gap. No refinement of a stock-return distribution captures an unmodeled fifth attendance year.
2. **Deposit interruption:** $52,000 plus $500/month for eight pre-entry years funds the same $100,000 goal. Pausing the final 12 deposits leaves a $6,000 gap. The current nominal household cap is not a stochastic model of contribution capacity.
3. **Deposit policy:** Two earmarked students, each owing $200 over one year, have different enrollment dates. A has $100 and can receive one deposit; B saves over the following year. Under a $100/month cap, fixed $50/$50 first-month deposits leave A $50 short while B ends with excess. Depositing $100 to A and zero to B in that first month, then continuing B's $50/month schedule, funds both. No money transfers between accounts occur. Fixed-share failure is therefore not proof that every policy under the cap fails.

The full dated ledger outputs and assertions are in the support artifact. Small floating-point residuals are rounded here to dollars. These examples are not estimates of how common these situations are.

### Price-process identification is still unresolved

The source audit shows that excluding analyst-corrected records changes the evaluated coverage by at most 0.50 percentage point. That makes those few flags an unconvincing explanation of the shortfall in this sample. It does not prove every `R` price is economically comparable or error-free.

The large within-cell error share is not evidence that the same school has a permanent higher growth rate. Adjacent growth windows can share endpoint measurement error; gapped comparisons remain mostly weak/negative, but that still does not uniquely identify a mean-reverting latent price process. Published-price resets, a change in housing/meal assumptions and real tuition policy can generate different behavior with similar summary correlations.

The existing fixed nominal cost transform in `cost-trends.js` is also different from a structural “future CPI plus real school growth” model. Its cost donors are matched to broad macro histories through `costDonorIndices`, including inflation-weighted substitutes when no same-year donor exists. This is a useful explicit assumption, not 70 years of observed joint school/market data. Validate the resulting cross-variable and horizon behavior; do not infer it from marginal moment checks alone.

Time-box the committed 30-event source trace, record unresolved causes, and stop short of “correcting” large prices merely to make a model fit. This investigation should run alongside scenario-definition work, not indefinitely block it.

## 6. Recommended model architecture

Keep three outputs distinct within one Calculate flow:

1. **Contractual/entered cash-flow assessment:** what must be paid, when, what funds are available and which bill first fails. Known entered costs and explicit alternative paths are central.
2. **Robustness assessment:** how the same policy performs under named alternative assumptions and life-event stresses, without probability weights unless there is a defensible distribution. Show specific vulnerability and rescue actions, not an invented calibrated readiness score.
3. **Conditional stochastic assessment:** ranges and success frequencies under a stated, versioned economic process, with horizon/source support visible. Keep the existing gauge, but do not let it imply broader validation than exists.

A world should supply joint returns, inflation and any explicitly assumed cost/support shocks. An education path should supply attendance episodes and bill definitions. A policy should supply contribution/allocation decisions using information available at that date. The ledger compiles them into outcomes. This separation permits future adaptive policies without giving them hindsight about the future path.

For costs, benchmark a last-real-price process and a damped sector model before a more complex candidate. A small level/trend model with temporary school deviations and a separately motivated observation layer is a candidate class, not a selected replacement. Do not stack it with old residual/spread layers. Use an offline reference implementation and known-process tests; export a compact sampler only if its complete cost and funding evidence qualifies. Fewer parameters and stronger baselines matter more than another elaborate curve family.

For household decisions, start by comparing fixed shares with a simple dated-priority contribution policy under the same cap, without transferring existing earmarked balances. A small fixed-scenario optimization can independently benchmark it later. Do not jump directly to reinforcement learning or a general lifetime-consumption optimizer.

## 7. Are we choosing the correct scenarios?

**Partly. Keep the existing price/ledger experiment, but add a distinct suite organized around decisions and failure mechanisms.** The [scenario plan](scenario-design-plan.md) defines the proposed matrix and gates. Highest-value additions are:

- A known near-entry offer with 1–4 remaining attendance years, plus one extra year and a grant-renewal alternative.
- An unknown future institution, compared with a budget class or maximum parent commitment rather than a falsely known exact school's long-run trajectory.
- A public two-year path, an explicit two-plus-two transfer path and living-at-home versus on-campus alternatives; their schedules and total costs must differ.
- A 6/12-month savings interruption, lost/delayed gift, reduced student income and an aid-renewal shock; one compound adverse case with a market drawdown, with no invented joint frequency.
- Sibling overlap with an actually binding cap, near a decision boundary, including a comparison of fixed and dated-priority deposits.
- Shortfall timing both before and during attendance, plus an adaptation rule such as lowering the cost ceiling or revising deposits at annual reviews using only then-available information.

Do not apply every extreme to every plan in a huge Cartesian grid. Use targeted cases, selected interactions and separate realistic alternatives from deliberately severe stresses. Scenario ranges below are proposed engineering test values, not population estimates or financial recommendations.

## 8. Evaluation needs stronger comparators

Preserve proper interval scores and known-budget normalization. Add last-observed-real-price and ordinary trend baselines for costs. For funding, compare with a deterministic dated-bill plan and an origin-known funding-ratio classifier, alongside the existing earlier-completed base rate. The present review has not established which baseline will win.

For decision-region evaluation, choose opening savings/deposit levels at an origin using only the baseline forecast/solver—for example levels around its predicted 80/90/95% thresholds—and freeze them before observing the outcome. Evaluate all candidates on that same cohort and also retain the original broad/easy grid. Do not select plans using actual shortfalls or shift each candidate's test population to flatter it. If plans are selected using the baseline, label that design choice and report a separately chosen broad cohort.

Report by horizon, investment policy, coverage goal, school-history type, pathway and binding-cap state. Add actual dollar shortfall severity and deposit/rescue cost to probability/interval scores. A model that raises success by requiring impossible deposits has not improved the decision. A model that changes a binary result for a one-dollar miss should not automatically outrank one that materially reduces a severe gap.

Keep chronological completion cutoffs, source identities, common random numbers and candidate-specific funding tests. An origin bootstrap that resamples overlapping histories remains descriptive, as currently labeled. Neither more schools nor more Monte Carlo paths creates a new macroeconomic decade. Proper scoring [S11] and a robust experiment design [S6/S7] address different questions; use both.

## 9. Revised execution order

| Stage | Deliverable | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Explicit goal/world/path/policy contract and scenario registry | Every case names the decision, coverage, dates, support assumptions and information available to its policy; no unimplemented UI claims |
| B, parallel | Fixed 30-event source trace | Version/definition comparisons and unresolved causes recorded; no open-ended search for a perfectly clean panel |
| C | Strong simple baselines and decision-region evaluation design | Origin-only plan selection, binding-cap and attendance-duration checks, controlled examples and complete ledger comparisons |
| D | One newly committed cost-candidate protocol and offline reference | Known-process identification/variance/covariance checks; no residual stacking or final-score tuning |
| E | Matched cost, funding and policy evaluation | Proper scores plus decision losses, supported horizons and retained failed comparisons; production activation remains separate |
| F | Prospective evidence and periodic review | Original forecasts preserved; reached comparable outcomes source-backed; no unsupported long-horizon claim |

A–C can improve useful planning and reveal model value before another cost candidate is promoted. The fixed trace remains valuable, but it is not the only next work. This is a proposed priority update, not a change to existing frozen promotion rules or a declaration that the new scenarios are implemented.
