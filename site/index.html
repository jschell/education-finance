How the planner works

A funding goal, not a forecast

Success means every expense in your selected coverage goal is paid when due. A tuition-only result does not mean living expenses are funded.

Economic cases & historical replay

One calculation resamples aligned historical market returns, tuition inflation, and general inflation. It also compares actual historical periods and provides a fixed-rate reference.

Keep a portable backup

Changes save automatically to this browser. Export regularly for a backup. Browser data can be cleared. There is no cross-device sync or account login.

What the latest historical checks show

College-cost uncertainty remains unresolved. These are retrospective tests of published prices using fictional plans; the number of schools and simulations does not increase the number of independent histories.

In fixed-school tests, the model's middle 80% cost band contained actual four-year bills in 76% of immediate-entry cases, 20% of cases with a four-year wait, and 10% with an eight-year wait. Missed bills were below the forecast band, indicating overestimated costs in those periods.

Older public-sector national averages show the opposite direction: all 14 tested bills after an 18-year wait exceeded the benchmark's upper band. Those cases share seven overlapping start years and only one nonoverlapping complete outcome window. They test costs only, not a family's funding probability.

The latest evaluation fitted each cost model's own bias and uncertainty. The resulting selection policy did not meet the declared improvement threshold, so existing assumptions are retained. These checks do not establish that a future funding percentage is calibrated. Read the full calibration results and limitations.
